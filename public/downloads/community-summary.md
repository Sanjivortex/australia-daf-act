## Paragraph version

Australia's Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 became law on 8 April 2026 and, on ASIC's latest timetable, commences on 8 April 2027. It creates two new financial products: digital asset platforms, where a business holds digital tokens for customers, and tokenised custody platforms, where a business holds an asset and issues one redeemable token for it. Operators will generally need an Australian financial services licence and must follow ASIC standards, platform rules and retail disclosure rules. Existing platforms have until 7 October 2027 to apply and can keep operating while ASIC decides.

Not everything is caught. Small digital asset platforms (no more than $10 million in transactions over 12 months and $5,000 per customer, with a notice to ASIC) do not need a licence, and public blockchains that meet a three-part test are not financial products. ASIC is consulting on its draft standards and guidance until 27 November 2026 and plans to finalise them in March 2027.

For issuers tokenising through Tokeniser, the key questions are who controls the tokens or keys, whether a separate asset sits behind each token, and whether the product is already regulated, for example as a managed investment scheme. ASIC's draft guide says native tokenised units in a unit trust would generally be a managed investment scheme rather than a tokenised custody platform. Our read: Redbelly's identity and eligibility tools line up well with the Act's focus on clear eligibility rules and good records, but the legal duties stay with the licensed operator or issuer. Some questions remain open, including whether Redbelly counts as public digital token infrastructure.

This is general information, not legal or financial advice, and it does not say that any project or issuer is licensed or compliant. Full explainer and sources: https://australia-daf-act.vercel.app
